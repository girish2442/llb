1. Principles of translation

  1. Accuracy: preserve the source meaning; do not add or omit legal content.
  2. Completeness: translate headings, provisos, explanations, dates, amounts and qualifications.
  3. Clarity: use natural target-language grammar without changing legal effect.
  4. Consistency: translate the same defined term consistently throughout.
  5. Register: use formal legal language, not casual speech.
  6. Context: one word may have different meanings in ordinary and legal usage.
  7. Neutrality: do not improve, soften or strengthen the author's legal position.
  8. Review: compare source and target sentence by sentence; check names, dates, section numbers and negations.
Important: A translation exercise is not an opportunity to paraphrase away a legal condition. Words such as “unless”, “provided that”, “shall not” and “subject to” can change rights and liabilities.

2. A practical legal translation method

  1. Read the entire passage before translating.
  2. Identify parties, defined terms, legal action and operative verbs.
  3. Mark conditions, exceptions, provisos, negatives and time limits.
  4. Translate sentence by sentence, retaining logical order.
  5. Use a consistent glossary for legal terms.
  6. Read the target version independently for grammar and naturalness.
  7. Back-check against the source for omissions or altered legal effect.

4. Translation exercises – original practice passages

Exercise A – translate into Kannada: “The applicant shall submit the documents within thirty days. If the applicant fails to do so, the authority may reject the application after giving an opportunity to explain the delay.”
Exercise B – translate into Kannada: “The parties entered into an agreement voluntarily. However, the agreement shall not affect any right that cannot lawfully be waived.”
Exercise C – translate into Kannada: “The Court considered the evidence, framed the issue and dismissed the appeal for the reasons stated in the judgment.”

5. Translation back into English – practice

Translate a short teacher-supplied Kannada legal passage into English. Retain the identity of parties, date, amount, statutory reference, condition and relief. Avoid word-for-word translation where it produces incorrect English; preserve legal meaning instead.

Practice checklist

  1. Translate Exercise A and underline the time limit, consequence and procedural safeguard.
  2. Translate Exercise B and identify the qualification introduced by “however”.
  3. Translate Exercise C and preserve the sequence of judicial actions.
  4. Take a short paragraph from a reported judgment and translate it, preserving facts, issue and holding.
  5. Prepare a glossary of 20 terms from the legal passage selected by your teacher.

Suggested marking: legal meaning 40%; completeness 20%; terminology consistency 15%; grammar/naturalness 15%; punctuation and presentation 10%.

Quick revision

  • Translate meaning, not isolated words.
  • Preserve legal actors, actions, dates, amounts and section numbers.
  • Never omit a negative, proviso, exception or time limit.
  • Use consistent equivalents for defined terms.
  • Keep the distinction between may / shall and right / remedy.
  • Review the translated text against the original before submission.
