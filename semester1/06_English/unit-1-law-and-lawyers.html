1. About the author – M. K. Gandhi

Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948), widely known as Mahatma Gandhi, was an Indian lawyer, writer and leader of the freedom movement. He studied law in London and was called to the Bar before beginning his legal career.

His experiences as a lawyer and public figure informed his reflections on truth, fairness, service, self-discipline and public responsibility. Distinguish facts from his biography from the specific arguments he makes in the assigned passage.

Exam tip: Keep the author's biography brief. In an answer about the lesson, focus on the ideas found in the prescribed text rather than writing a long life history.

2. Reading guide – themes to identify

Use this checklist while reading the actual chapter. These are reading prompts, not quotations or a substitute for the lesson.

  1. Purpose: What question or issue about law and lawyers is Gandhi addressing?
  2. Law as a profession: What does the author say about the role and conduct of legal practitioners?
  3. Truth and justice: Identify any connection the text makes between truthfulness, fairness and legal work.
  4. Service and responsibility: What duties does the author describe? Record the exact explanation from the lesson.
  5. Personal experience: Note incidents Gandhi uses and what each example is intended to show.

4. Preliminary exam question bank

Practice questions based on the lesson title and learning objectives; not verified previous-year questions. Answers depending on Gandhi's exact examples or wording will be finalised after checking the prescribed chapter.
Q1. Who is the author of “Law and Lawyers”?

The lesson is attributed in the KSLU English syllabus reference to M. K. Gandhi, also known as Mahatma Gandhi. He was a lawyer, writer and leader of India's freedom movement.

Q2. Why is the author's experience as a lawyer relevant?

Gandhi's legal education and professional experience provide background for understanding his reflections on law and lawyers. Connect this background to the specific incidents and arguments in the prescribed lesson.

Q3. Explain the importance of professional ethics in legal practice.

Professional ethics are standards that guide a lawyer's conduct. They support honesty, fairness, responsibility and respect for the legal process. A lawyer should act competently, avoid misleading the court and respect professional duties. Add the particular view or example given by Gandhi in the prescribed lesson.

Q4. Write a short note on the relationship between law and justice.

Law provides rules and procedures for resolving disputes and regulating conduct. Justice is concerned with fairness and proper treatment. Support this answer with the relevant passage from the prescribed lesson when available.

Q5. What qualities should a responsible lawyer possess?

A responsible lawyer should have legal knowledge, clear communication, diligence, honesty, integrity and respect for the court and client. The final answer should identify which qualities Gandhi specifically emphasises and explain his examples from the text.

Q6. Write a paragraph on the social responsibility of a lawyer.

A lawyer helps people understand and use the law. The lawyer should assist the client while respecting the court, legal procedure and the interests of justice. Add the author's specific position from the lesson before using this as a final examination answer.

5. Language and comprehension practice

A. Paragraph writing

Topic: The qualities of a good lawyer.

Structure: Opening sentence → knowledge and communication → honesty and professional conduct → duty to client and court → conclusion.

B. Comprehension method

  1. Read the passage once to understand its main idea.
  2. Read the questions and identify the relevant part.
  3. Answer in your own words unless asked to quote.
  4. Keep answers direct and grammatically correct.
  5. Do not add facts unsupported by the passage.

C. Grammar check

1. “A lawyers are officers of the court.”
Correction: “Lawyers are officers of the court.” The subject is plural, so do not use “a”.
2. “The lawyer have a duty to the client.”
Correction: “The lawyer has a duty to the client.” A singular subject takes “has”.
3. “He is senior than me.”
Correction: “He is senior to me.” The adjective “senior” is followed by “to”.
