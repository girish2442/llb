1. Petitions

A petition is a formal written request to a court or public authority for relief. A basic academic format includes: (1) name of forum; (2) parties and status; (3) brief material facts in numbered paragraphs; (4) grounds; (5) relief/prayer; (6) place/date/signature; (7) verification/affidavit where required by applicable rules.

A classroom model is not a filing-ready pleading. Real petitions must comply with the relevant statute, court rules, limitation and prescribed forms.
Model structure (illustrative)

BEFORE THE [NAME OF AUTHORITY]
In the matter of: [Applicant] v. [Respondent]
Petition seeking consideration of a representation

1. The applicant states that …
2. The relevant events occurred on …
3. The applicant submitted a representation dated …
4. No decision has been communicated despite …

Grounds: The applicant seeks a reasoned decision in accordance with applicable law and fair procedure.

Prayer: The applicant respectfully requests that the authority consider and decide the representation in accordance with law.

Place / Date / Signature / Verification as required.

2. Notices: public notice and legal notice

A public notice communicates information to the public generally (for example, a change of address or invitation for claims). A legal notice communicates a legal claim or demand to a particular person before proposed legal action, where appropriate or required.

PartContent
Heading“Legal Notice” or “Public Notice”
PartiesSender and addressee / affected public
FactsDates, transaction and relevant events

Demand / informationAction requested, how and by when; or information being announced

Model legal notice – unpaid invoice (academic example)

LEGAL NOTICE
Date: 14 October 2026
To: ABC Traders, [address]
Under instructions from my client, I state that goods were supplied under invoice no. 14 dated 1 August 2026 for the agreed price of ₹25,000. The invoice remains unpaid despite reminders dated 10 and 20 September 2026. You are called upon to pay the outstanding amount within 15 days of receipt of this notice, failing which my client may pursue remedies available in law. This notice is issued without prejudice to other lawful rights and remedies.

3. Refutation – answering the other side

Refutation is a reasoned response to an argument or allegation. State the opponent's point fairly, identify the weakness, provide contrary facts or reasoning, address evidence and conclude. Do not merely say “this is false”.

Example: Claim: “The notice was received late, so the party should automatically lose.” Refutation: “The date of receipt alone does not establish that the claim is barred. The applicable limitation provision, the date on which the cause of action arose and any legally recognised exclusion or extension must be examined before reaching that conclusion.”

4. Essay writing on legal topics

Possible topics: access to justice; legal aid; rule of law; gender justice; environmental protection; rights and duties of citizens; technology in courts. Define the issue, explain the legal/social context, discuss benefits and limitations, and conclude with a balanced view. Do not cite a section or judgment unless certain of its relevance.

5. Comprehension of legal content

Identify defined terms, conditions, exceptions, consequences and the authority/party concerned. Distinguish “shall” (often mandatory, context matters) from “may” (often discretionary, context matters). Read provisos and explanations with the main rule.

7. Cohesive legal devices

Use legal connectors precisely: whereas (background/contrast), therefore/accordingly (result), provided that (condition), subject to (qualification), notwithstanding (override), for the avoidance of doubt (clarification), in view of (reason), furthermore (addition). Avoid archaic phrases where plain English is clearer.

Practice exercises

  1. Draft an academic petition requesting a public authority to decide a pending representation.
  2. Prepare a public notice inviting claims relating to a lost document. Do not publish real personal data.
  3. Draft a legal notice demanding payment of an overdue invoice using the facts in Unit IV notes.
  4. Refute the statement: “A person who has not signed a document can never be bound by it.”
  5. Write an essay on “Legal aid as an instrument of social justice”.
  6. Read a short statutory extract provided by your teacher and identify the main rule, conditions, proviso and consequence.
Refutation prompt: “All delays in filing a case are excusable if the litigant says they were unaware of the law.”

Model answer / answer guide

Refutation: Lack of awareness does not automatically excuse delay. The applicable limitation law governs the time for bringing proceedings. A court may condone delay only where the relevant statute permits it and the required grounds are established. The facts, explanation for delay and governing provision must therefore be examined. The statement is too broad and legally inaccurate.

Petition marking points: correct forum and parties; concise facts; clear grounds; precise prayer; verification where required; respectful language.

Notice marking points: sender/addressee; date; factual chronology; legal basis stated cautiously; specific demand and reasonable deadline; lawful consequence; signature.

Quick revision

  • Petition: forum → parties → facts → grounds → prayer → verification.
  • Notice: identify parties, facts, claim, demand and deadline.
  • Refutation: fairly state the claim, then answer with reasons and facts.
  • Legal essays require a balanced argument and accurate terminology.
  • Read provisos, exceptions and defined terms carefully.
  • Use cohesive legal devices only where their legal meaning fits.
