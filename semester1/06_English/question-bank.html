Problem-based questions

  1. Grammar: Correct: “Each of the parties have submitted their evidence and the judge were satisfied.”
  2. Voice: Convert: “The authority rejected the application after considering the documents.”
  3. Reported speech: Report: The advocate said, “I will file the reply tomorrow.”
  4. Précis: Summarise a passage supplied by the examiner to one-third of its length.
  5. Report: Draft a press report about a legal literacy camp using the facts supplied in the question.
  6. Petition: Prepare a petition asking an authority to decide a pending representation.
  7. Notice: Draft a legal notice demanding payment of an overdue invoice.
  8. Refutation: Refute: “A person can never be held liable unless they personally signed the agreement.”
  9. Translation: Translate a legal passage retaining a proviso, limitation period and negative condition.

Model answers – selected

2-mark: What is a cohesive device? A word or phrase that connects ideas and shows their logical relationship, such as however, therefore, moreover and consequently.

5-mark: Public notice and legal notice. A public notice communicates information to the public generally. A legal notice is directed to a particular person or entity to communicate a claim, demand or legal position before further action. A legal notice usually identifies the parties, material facts, demand and time for compliance. The legal effect depends on the relevant law and circumstances.

Grammar correction: “Each of the parties has submitted the required evidence, and the judge was satisfied.” (Alternative pronoun choices depend on intended meaning.)

Passive: “The application was rejected by the authority after the documents had been considered.”

Reported speech: “The advocate said that he would file the reply the next day.”

Refutation: The statement is too broad. A signature may be important evidence of assent, but legal obligations can arise through other recognised modes depending on the applicable law and facts. The nature of the transaction, authority, conduct, statutory rules and evidence must be examined.
