1. My Search for Truth – Radhakrishnan’s early life and making of a philosopher Prose

Source-based study points

  • Born on 5 September 1888 at Tiruttani; childhood also connected with Tirupati.
  • Studied at Lutheran Mission High School, Voorhees College and Madras Christian College.
  • Christian missionary criticism of Hinduism encouraged a closer study of Hindu philosophy.
  • Swami Vivekananda’s example strengthened confidence in Indian thought.
  • His study of Vedanta and its ethics influenced his philosophical development.
Exam focus: early life, factors shaping his character and why he took up the study of Hindu philosophy.
Revise: (1) Where and when was Radhakrishnan born? (2) Name the three educational institutions. (3) What prompted his closer study of Hindu philosophy? (4) How did Vivekananda influence him?
Model answer – Factors that shaped his character (10 marks):

Dr. S. Radhakrishnan’s character was shaped by his religious surroundings, education and search for truth. He was born into an orthodox Hindu family and grew up in an atmosphere connected with temples and pilgrimage. His shy and reflective nature encouraged reading and study. His education in Christian institutions exposed him to the Bible and criticism of Hindu beliefs. This encouraged him to examine Hindu philosophy carefully rather than accept or reject criticism blindly. The example of Swami Vivekananda strengthened his confidence in Indian thought. His teachers and philosophical studies further developed his intellectual outlook. His faith in providence also influenced him. Thus, family, education, critical inquiry and spiritual faith shaped him into a philosopher.

2. My Search for Truth – Religion, God, philosophy and modern civilization Prose

Source-based study points

  • Religion is presented as experience and a way of life, not merely beliefs or rituals.
  • The note emphasises tolerance and rejects the idea that one religion has a monopoly on truth.
  • Philosophy seeks to understand life and reality as a whole; reason and intuition both matter.
  • The modern-civilization discussion highlights material progress without corresponding inner or spiritual growth.
Revise: (1) How does Radhakrishnan understand religion? (2) Why does he emphasise tolerance? (3) State four points on the aim of philosophy. (4) Explain “lack of spiritual note in modern civilization”.
Model answer – Aim of philosophy (10 marks):

According to Radhakrishnan, philosophy seeks to understand the meaning of life and reality as a whole. It is not merely an academic exercise. It uses reason and also recognises intuition and experience. Philosophy should guide conduct and become a way of life. It gives religion a rational foundation, while religion gives philosophy living content. Philosophy also helps reconcile Eastern and Western thought. Its aim is to develop a deeper understanding of life and guide human beings towards spiritual insight and right conduct.

3. Law and Lawyers – Gandhi’s preparation for England, the Bar and first years of practice Prose

Source-based study points

  • Gandhi considered going to England to study law and become a barrister; his mother worried about religious restrictions.
  • He promised not to touch wine, women or meat; a family adviser helped reassure his mother.
  • He sailed from Bombay on 4 September 1888, studied in London and joined the Inner Temple.
  • He was called to the Bar in June 1891 and returned to India.
  • Early practice involved hesitation, lack of confidence in court, financial difficulty and moves between Bombay and Rajkot.
  • The source names the Small Causes Court matter, Mamibai’s case, the Political Agent and Pherozeshah Mehta’s advice.
Revise: What three assurances did Gandhi give his mother? When did he sail? What was the Inner Temple? Mention three difficulties in early practice.
Model answer – Gandhi’s preparation for England (10 marks):

After passing his matriculation examination, Gandhi considered going to England to study law and become a barrister. His mother was worried that he might adopt habits contrary to their religious beliefs. Gandhi promised that he would not touch wine, women or meat. A family adviser helped reassure his mother, and she gave permission. Gandhi sailed from Bombay on 4 September 1888. In England, he studied law, joined the Inner Temple and prepared for the examinations. He was called to the Bar in June 1891. His preparation shows the importance he attached to his mother’s trust and to keeping his promises.

4. Law and Lawyers – Gandhi in South Africa and Dada Abdulla’s case Prose

Source-based study points

  • Gandhi accepted an offer from Dada Abdulla & Co. to work in South Africa for a year, with a first-class return fare and £105.
  • The dispute involved Dada Abdulla and his relative Tyeb Haji Khan Muhammad (Tyeb Sheth), business transactions, accounts and promissory notes.
  • Gandhi studied the papers, examined accounts, translated Gujarati correspondence and grasped the facts.
  • He saw the danger of prolonged litigation and persuaded the parties to refer the dispute to arbitration.
  • The settlement involved payment by instalments and avoided financial ruin.
  • The lesson: a lawyer’s true function is to unite parties; facts are central to law.
Important: Dada Abdulla was Gandhi’s client and the plaintiff; Tyeb Sheth was the defendant. Do not reverse the parties.
Revise: Identify the parties. What was the nature of the claim? How did Gandhi prepare? Why did he prefer arbitration?
Model answer – Dada Abdulla’s case (10 marks):

Gandhi went to South Africa on an offer from Dada Abdulla & Co. to assist in a case involving a claim of about £40,000 against Tyeb Sheth, a relative of Dada Abdulla. The dispute arose from business transactions and promissory notes. Gandhi carefully studied the papers, examined the accounts and translated Gujarati correspondence. This helped him understand the facts and interests of both parties. He realised that prolonged litigation would consume money and damage family relations. He persuaded the parties to refer the matter to arbitration. The dispute was settled, and payment was arranged by instalments. Gandhi learned that a lawyer should help unite parties and that facts are the foundation of a case.

Model answer – Gandhi’s legal philosophy (10 marks):

Gandhi believed that law should serve truth and justice. A lawyer should not use dishonest methods or encourage unnecessary disputes. The lawyer’s duty is to understand the facts, advise the client honestly and help parties reach a fair settlement where possible. Professional success should not be achieved through sharp practice. The Rustomji episode is used in the study material to illustrate truth and integrity at the Bar. Gandhi’s approach places ethical responsibility above fees or victory in a case. His legal philosophy treats the lawyer as a guide towards justice and reconciliation.

8. Tenses, articles and prepositions Grammar

Tense quick reference

FormPresentPastFuture
SimpleHe writesHe wroteHe will write
ContinuousHe is writingHe was writingHe will be writing
PerfectHe has writtenHe had writtenHe will have written
Perfect continuousHe has been writingHe had been writingHe will have been writing

Articles

  • A before a consonant sound; an before a vowel sound: a university, an hour.
  • The for something already identified, unique things, superlatives and certain names/institutions.
  • No article before most proper names and with some abstract/uncountable nouns used generally.

Prepositions

  • Time: at 9 o’clock; on Monday; in May; since 2019; for six years.
  • Place/movement: at a point, in an enclosed/large place, to for direction, into for movement inside.
  • Fixed pairs: angry with a person, fond of, associate with, accused of, charged with, comply with, superior to, liable for, agree with/to.
Fill in: (1) He is ___ honest man. (2) The hearing begins ___ 10 a.m. (3) She has practised law ___ 2020. (4) The accused was charged ___ theft. (5) We do not go to college ___ Sundays.
Answers: (1) an (2) at (3) since (4) with (5) on. Choose articles by sound; learn preposition combinations as fixed expressions.
9. Types of sentences and transformation of sentences Grammar

Structure and function

  • Simple: one main clause.
  • Compound: two or more main clauses joined by coordinating conjunctions.
  • Complex: one main clause and one or more subordinate clauses.
  • Assertive: states a fact; interrogative: asks; imperative: commands/requests; exclamatory: expresses feeling.
TaskExample
Simple → compoundBeing tired, he slept. → He was tired and so he slept.
Simple → complexIn spite of running fast, he missed the train. → Though he ran fast, he missed the train.
Exclamatory → assertiveWhat a great win! → It is a very great win.
Affirmative → negativeHe is rich. → He is not poor.
Transform: (1) Being tired, she went to bed (compound). (2) Though he was poor, he was honest (simple). (3) What a beautiful day! (assertive). (4) Always speak the truth (negative). (5) He is hardworking. He is sincere (both…and).
Answers: (1) She was tired and so she went to bed. (2) In spite of his poverty, he was honest. (3) It is a very beautiful day. (4) Never tell a lie. (5) He is both hardworking and sincere.
10. Active and passive voice Grammar

In active voice, the subject performs the action. In passive voice, the object becomes the subject; use the appropriate form of be + past participle.

TenseActivePassive
Simple presentHe reads novels.Novels are read by him.
Present continuousTom is building a house.A house is being built by Tom.
Present perfectSomeone has stolen my bag.My bag has been stolen.
Simple pastPeter wrote this essay.This essay was written by Peter.
Past continuousShe was singing a song.A song was being sung by her.
Past perfectHe had signed the deed.The deed had been signed by him.
Simple futureI shall call you.You will be called by me.
ModalHe can do this work.This work can be done by him.
  • Imperative: “Post the letter.” → “Let the letter be posted.”
  • Two objects: either object may become subject where grammatical.
  • Keep the preposition: “They laughed at him.” → “He was laughed at.”
  • Legal writing often uses passive voice when the act or affected person matters more than the actor.
Change voice: (1) The policeman caught the thief. (2) Give the order. (3) Someone has stolen my bag. (4) Do they speak English? (5) The Chief Guest distributed the prizes.
Answers: (1) The thief was caught by the policeman. (2) Let the order be given. (3) My bag has been stolen. (4) Is English spoken by them? (5) The prizes were distributed by the Chief Guest.
11. Reported speech – direct and indirect Grammar

Four changes

  1. Reporting verb and joining word: said/told + that; asked + if/whether for yes/no; asked + wh-word for wh-questions; ordered/requested + to-infinitive for commands.
  2. Tense usually moves one step back when the reporting verb is past.
  3. Pronouns change according to speaker, listener and person spoken about.
  4. Time/place words: now→then; today→that day; tomorrow→the next day; yesterday→the previous day; here→there.
DirectIndirect
is / am / arewas / were
writeswrote
is writingwas writing
has writtenhad written
will / can / maywould / could / might
No backshift is normally needed when the reporting verb is present or the statement expresses a universal truth.
Convert: (1) He said, “How are you?” (2) She said, “I have written a novel.” (3) The teacher said, “Boys, work hard.” (4) He said, “I am glad to be here.” (5) The gentleman said, “Can you lend me your pen?”
Answers: (1) He asked me how I was. (2) She said that she had written a novel. (3) The teacher advised the boys to work hard. (4) He said that he was glad to be there. (5) The gentleman asked me whether I could lend him my pen.

15. Formal correspondence – invitation, request, enquiry, complaint, job application and letter to the editor Writing

Formal letter layout

  1. Sender’s address at top left.
  2. Date.
  3. Receiver’s designation and address.
  4. Subject line stating the purpose.
  5. Salutation: Sir/Madam or Dear Sir/Madam.
  6. Body: opening purpose, relevant details, request/action and closing.
  7. Complimentary close and signature/name.
Letter typeEssential content
InvitationOccasion, date, time, venue, chief guest and RSVP.
RequestWhat is requested, why, duration and polite closing.
EnquiryProduct/service, specific questions, details requested and reply request.
ComplaintTransaction, defect/problem, evidence, desired remedy and time limit.
Job applicationPost, qualifications, experience, suitability and interview request.
Letter to editorPublic problem, local impact, suggested remedy and appeal to authorities.

The Editor
The Times of India
Hubballi

Subject: Irregular power supply in Vidyanagar

Sir/Madam,

Through the columns of your newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the electricity department and the authorities to the irregular power supply in Vidyanagar, Hubballi.

Residents have been facing unscheduled power cuts several times a day, often lasting three to four hours. The voltage also fluctuates when supply returns. Students find it difficult to study, patients and elderly residents face inconvenience, and small businesses suffer losses.

I request the authorities to publish a schedule of necessary load-shedding, inspect the overloaded transformer and repair the distribution network promptly. I hope this letter will draw the attention of the concerned officials.

Yours faithfully,
A. B. C.

16. Comprehension of unseen passages and words often confused Comprehension

Method for unseen passages

  1. Read questions first, then read the passage with a purpose.
  2. Mark the lines that answer each question.
  3. Answer in your own words in one to three complete sentences.
  4. For “why” questions, give the reason from the passage.
  5. Do not add outside facts or opinions.
  6. For a title, choose a short phrase covering the whole passage.

Passages represented in uploaded notes

  • January 2026: computer – definition, usefulness, input/process/output, storage and programs.
  • January 2026: poverty – attitudes to wealth, children of the rich, family affection and qualities developed through poverty.
  • June 2025: pollutants and science – DDT, smog, fallout, interference with nature and risks of incomplete knowledge.
Model answers – Computer passage themes:
  1. A computer is an electronic device that accepts data, processes information according to instructions and produces the desired output.
  2. It is useful because it can perform work quickly and accurately.
  3. The user gives instructions through input devices such as a keyboard, mouse or scanner. The CPU processes the input and produces output through a screen or printer.
  4. Information can be stored on a hard disk or other storage device for future use.
  5. Programs are sequences of commands that tell the computer what to do.
