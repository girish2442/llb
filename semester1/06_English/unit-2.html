1. Articles: a, an, the and zero article

A is used before a consonant sound; an before a vowel sound. The sound, not the first letter, decides: a university (yu sound), an hour (silent h). Use the for a particular or already identified person/thing, unique things, superlatives and many geographical names.

RuleExample
First mention / non-specific singular count nounA lawyer entered the court.
Specific later referenceThe lawyer addressed the judge.
Vowel soundAn advocate; an FIR; an honest person.
General plural or uncountable nounLawyers must act ethically. Justice matters.
Unique referenceThe Constitution of India.

2. Parts of speech

  • Noun: names a person/place/thing/idea – court, justice.
  • Pronoun: replaces a noun – she, they, who.
  • Verb: action/state – argues, is, decided.
  • Adjective: describes a noun – valid contract.
  • Adverb: modifies a verb/adjective – argued clearly.
  • Preposition: shows relation – under the Act, before the court.
  • Conjunction: joins – and, but, because.
  • Interjection: expresses feeling – Oh! Alas!

4. Error identification: quick rules

  • Subject–verb agreement: The list of cases is ready (head noun list).
  • Consistent tense: Yesterday the court heard the matter.
  • Correct preposition: senior to, accused of, comply with, appeal against.
  • Pronoun agreement: Each party must file its reply.
  • Parallel structure: The lawyer must read, analyse and argue.
  • Avoid double negatives: I do not know anything.

5. Sentence types and transformation

Assertive: The court delivered judgment. Interrogative: Did the court deliver judgment? Imperative: File the reply. Exclamatory: What a fair decision!

Simple: On receiving notice, the party appeared. Compound: The party received notice and appeared. Complex: When the party received notice, it appeared.

Transformation must preserve meaning. Example: Though he was ill, he attended court → He was ill, yet he attended court.

6. Active and passive voice

Active: subject performs action. Passive: subject receives action. Pattern: object + suitable form of be + past participle (+ by-agent). Example: The judge pronounced the order → The order was pronounced by the judge.

Only transitive verbs normally take passive forms. Do not force passive voice for verbs such as sleep or arrive.

7. Reported speech

When the reporting verb is in the past, tense often moves one step back: am/is → was; have done → had done; will → would. Change pronouns and time/place words where the context requires it. Universal truths normally remain unchanged.

DirectReported
He said, “I am ready.”He said that he was ready.
She said, “I have filed the appeal.”She said that she had filed the appeal.
He asked, “Are you the witness?”He asked whether I was the witness.
The judge said, “Do not interrupt.”The judge ordered them not to interrupt.

Legal words: accused (person charged), acquittal (finding of not guilty), affidavit (sworn written statement), appeal (request for higher-court review), counsel (lawyer/advice), decree (formal adjudication in a civil suit), injunction (court order to do/refrain), jurisdiction (authority to hear), plaintiff (civil claimant), respondent (party answering a petition/appeal), statute (legislative Act), testimony (evidence given by witness).

Practice exercises

A. Insert a/an/the/—
  1. He is ___ honest advocate.
  2. She studies at ___ university.
  3. ___ Constitution is the supreme law of India.
  4. ___ lawyers should maintain confidentiality.
  5. The witness gave ___ evidence.
B. Identify the part of speech of the bold word
  1. The court made an **order**.
  2. The judge may **order** a retrial.
  3. They gave a **clear** explanation.
  4. Please explain it **clearly**.
  5. The **appeal** is pending.
C. Correct the errors
  1. Each of the lawyers have filed a memo.
  2. She is senior than her colleague.
  3. The accused was charged for theft.
  4. The court have delivered its order.
  5. He did not know nothing about the case.
D. Transform
  1. Change to passive: The police arrested the suspect.
  2. Change to active: The petition was filed by the applicant.
  3. Make complex: He was tired, but he completed the work.
  4. Report: She said, “I will attend the hearing tomorrow.”
  5. Change to interrogative: The court accepted the evidence.

Quick revision

  • Articles depend on sound and specificity.
  • Identify a word's function in the sentence, not its dictionary category alone.
  • Check subject–verb agreement, tense and prepositions first in error correction.
  • Transformation must preserve meaning.
  • Passive = object + be + past participle.
  • Reported speech changes tense/pronouns/time references according to context.
