1. Audit basis and limitations

This audit compares the available Unit I–V notes and companion question banks against the KSLU 3-Year LL.B. Constitutional Law–I syllabus reproduced in the University's 2018–19 onward Appendix-IX syllabus PDF. The PDF identifies the course as Semester I and sets out the five unit topics. A KSLU circular introducing the revised three-year programme is included in the same PDF. The public syllabus page also describes the revised Appendix-VI A syllabus amended for new criminal laws from 2024–25; no change to Constitutional Law–I is indicated in the material inspected. The student's exact admission batch and any college-specific syllabus circular should still be confirmed before final sign-off.

The official syllabus is the controlling topic list. The existing Master Question List and HTML pages are study materials, not official syllabus documents. The audit does not treat probable questions as verified previous-year questions.

Official syllabus reference: KSLU 3-Year LL.B. syllabus PDF (Appendix-IX, Constitutional Law–I). Also see KSLU syllabus index and revised Appendix-VI A note.

3. Corrections and improvements made

  1. Unit IV syllabus scope now expressly lists Articles 23 and 24 under Right against Exploitation.
  2. Added a dedicated explanation of Article 23, including its application to private persons, economic compulsion and the public-purpose exception under Article 23(2).
  3. Added a dedicated explanation of Article 24, with the under-fourteen threshold and hazardous employment scope. The note cautions against describing it as a universal ban on every form of work by everyone under eighteen.
  4. Added leading cases: People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India (1982), Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India (1984), and M.C. Mehta v. State of Tamil Nadu (1996).
  5. Added an explicit secularism section and linked Articles 25–28 to S.R. Bommai (1994), Shirur Mutt (1954) and Bijoe Emmanuel (1986).
  6. Expanded Unit IV revision points and self-test for Articles 23–24 and secularism.
  7. Added a supplementary Unit IV question-bank section with short answers, medium answers, 10/15-mark frameworks, IRAC problems and case-law list.
  8. Updated subject index links to the delivered same-folder file names and added a link to this gap report. This fixes the previous mismatch where the dashboard pointed to a nested units/ directory not present in the material inspected.

4. Verification points before calling the subject fully complete

  • Confirm the student's admission batch and whether KSLU has issued a later Constitutional Law–I-specific syllabus amendment applicable to that batch.
  • Cross-check all case citations and ratios against the prescribed V.N. Shukla / M.P. Jain texts or authorised reports before memorising.
  • Re-check citizenship Sections 3–10 against the current Citizenship Act, amendments and applicable rules; the existing notes correctly warn that eligibility is date-sensitive.
  • Check the latest reservation/EWS constitutional developments and relevant judgments, particularly in Articles 15 and 16.
  • Check current Article 31C interpretation and property-acquisition procedure under Article 300A.
  • Verify each question against actual KSLU past papers before labelling it a PYQ. Existing pages appropriately describe their questions as practice/model questions.
  • Confirm with the prescribed course outline whether any additional lecturer-specific emphasis is expected.
