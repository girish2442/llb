Problem 1: Mistake of fact — Unit I · BNS s.14 and related general exceptions

Facts: A constable arrests X after checking an official warrant database that mistakenly shows a valid warrant. The constable acts honestly and follows required checks. Analyse possible protection.

Rule: Unit I · BNS s.14 and related general exceptions

Step-by-step analysis: Ask whether the constable was legally bound or in good-faith factual mistake believed so; identify authority, due care, factual basis and limits. Mistake of law is no defence. A genuine but careless assumption may fail good faith.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 2: Insanity — Unit I · BNS s.22

Facts: During a psychotic episode, A causes fatal injury and evidence shows A could not understand the nature or wrongfulness of the act at that time.

Rule: Unit I · BNS s.22

Step-by-step analysis: Focus on legal insanity at the time of act, not merely diagnosis. Examine capacity to know nature of act or that it was wrong/contrary to law. Apply evidence and burden principles; do not equate medical illness with legal unsoundness automatically.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 3: Private defence — Unit I · BNS ss.34–44

Facts: A attacks B with a knife. B disarms A; after A is helpless, B continues to stab A.

Rule: Unit I · BNS ss.34–44

Step-by-step analysis: B may use necessary force while danger exists. Once threat ends, continued stabbing may be retaliation and exceed the right. Examine apprehension, imminence, proportionality and statutory limits.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 4: Abetment — Unit II · BNS ss.45–60

Facts: A repeatedly urges B to burn C’s warehouse and supplies the fuel. B commits arson.

Rule: Unit II · BNS ss.45–60

Step-by-step analysis: Identify instigation and intentional aid. Prove A’s mental element and connection to offence. Consider liability/punishment provisions depending on whether offence is committed and statutory circumstances.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 5: Attempt vs preparation — Unit II · BNS s.62

Facts: A buys petrol and matches but is arrested before reaching the warehouse or taking any step to ignite it.

Rule: Unit II · BNS s.62

Step-by-step analysis: Purchasing materials ordinarily remains preparation. Determine whether A has crossed into a sufficiently proximate act. State that preparation may itself be separately criminalised in specific cases.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 6: Criminal conspiracy — Unit II · BNS s.61

Facts: A and B agree to rob a bank. They plan the route and allocate roles; police arrest them before the robbery.

Rule: Unit II · BNS s.61

Step-by-step analysis: Agreement to commit illegal act is central. No completed robbery is necessary for conspiracy. Prove meeting of minds, not mere parallel intention.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 7: Sexual consent — Unit II · BNS ss.63 onwards

Facts: A alleges consent because the complainant did not physically resist. Evidence shows fear and no voluntary agreement.

Rule: Unit II · BNS ss.63 onwards

Step-by-step analysis: Consent requires unequivocal voluntary agreement communicated by words/gestures or other verbal/non-verbal communication; absence of resistance alone does not imply consent. Apply age and statutory exceptions.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 8: Dowry death — Unit II · BNS s.80; evidence law presumption

Facts: A woman dies unnaturally within five years of marriage. Evidence shows dowry-linked harassment two days before death.

Rule: Unit II · BNS s.80; evidence law presumption

Step-by-step analysis: Check unnatural death within seven years, cruelty/harassment by husband/relative, dowry connection and “soon before” proximity. Only when foundational facts established does statutory presumption operate.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 9: Murder or culpable homicide — Unit III · BNS ss.100–105

Facts: A intentionally inflicts a deep knife wound on the chest; victim dies. A claims no intention to kill.

Rule: Unit III · BNS ss.100–105

Step-by-step analysis: Establish causation and culpable homicide; test murder clauses and exceptions. Intention to inflict the particular injury and its objective sufficiency may matter. Apply Virsa Singh and Rayavarapu Punnayya cautiously as IPC-era principles.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 10: Road death — Unit III · BNS s.106; check current operative text

Facts: A drives at high speed in a crowded market, ignores a red light and kills a pedestrian.

Rule: Unit III · BNS s.106; check current operative text

Step-by-step analysis: Determine whether conduct amounts to statutory rash/negligent act and caused death. Speed alone is not conclusive; surrounding facts and degree of negligence matter. Check applicable proviso and amendments/notifications.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 11: Hurt vs grievous hurt — Unit III · BNS ss.114–117

Facts: A punches B, causing pain and bruising. In another incident A fractures B’s arm.

Rule: Unit III · BNS ss.114–117

Step-by-step analysis: Both may be hurt; fracture is specifically grievous hurt. Identify medical evidence and statutory categories; punishment depends on weapon, intention and aggravating circumstances.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 12: Wrongful confinement — Unit III · BNS ss.126–127

Facts: A locks B in a room for three hours and prevents exit.

Rule: Unit III · BNS ss.126–127

Step-by-step analysis: Confinement is wrongful restraint within circumscribed limits. Prove voluntary obstruction and absence of lawful justification; duration may affect punishment but is not essential to basic definition.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 13: Unlawful assembly — Unit IV · BNS s.189 onwards

Facts: Six persons gather to protest. One member suddenly assaults a passer-by for a personal reason unrelated to the group object.

Rule: Unit IV · BNS s.189 onwards

Step-by-step analysis: Five persons alone is not enough: prove one of the prohibited common objects. Assess whether violence was in prosecution of common object and whether other members knew/continued participation.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Rule: Unit IV · BNS ss.227 onwards

Step-by-step analysis: Distinguish deliberate false evidence from mistake, faulty memory or inconsistency. Prove intentional falsity and materiality; identify applicable punishment/procedure.

Problem 15: Mischief and trespass — Unit IV · BNS ss.324 onwards

Facts: A enters land in another’s possession intending to intimidate the occupier, and breaks a gate causing damage.

Rule: Unit IV · BNS ss.324 onwards

Step-by-step analysis: Trespass focuses on entry/remain with specified intent and possession; mischief requires intention/knowledge of wrongful loss/damage and destruction/change diminishing value or utility. Separate ingredients can coexist.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 17: Robbery or extortion — Unit V · BNS ss.308–309

Facts: A threatens immediate injury unless B hands over his wallet at once.

Rule: Unit V · BNS ss.308–309

Step-by-step analysis: Extortion is fear-induced dishonest delivery; it becomes robbery where statutory instant fear/violence conditions are met. Identify exact mode and timing.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 18: Breach of trust vs cheating — Unit V · BNS ss.316, 318

Facts: A receives money as an agent to purchase goods, diverts it to personal use. In another case A obtains money by a false promise at inception.

Rule: Unit V · BNS ss.316, 318

Step-by-step analysis: First scenario: entrustment/dominion plus dishonest conversion suggests breach of trust. Second: deception and dishonest intention at inception may be cheating. Distinguish based on how possession was obtained.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.

Problem 19: Defamation and exception — Unit V · BNS s.356

Facts: A publishes a true statement about a public officer’s conduct, honestly believing it serves public good.

Rule: Unit V · BNS s.356

Step-by-step analysis: Prove publication/imputation and intent/knowledge/reason to believe reputation harmed, then test relevant statutory exception such as truth for public good and good faith. Truth alone is not an unlimited automatic defence.

Conclusion: Decide only after matching every statutory ingredient to the evidence; avoid automatic conclusions.
