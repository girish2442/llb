Answer practice is organised by marks. For 2 marks give a definition and section; for 5 marks explain the essentials; for 10/15 marks add statutory framework, ingredients, exceptions, cases, examples and conclusion.

2-mark definitions

Q1. Define theft and snatching.

Answer framework: Section 303: dishonest intention to take movable property out of another’s possession without consent, moving it to accomplish taking. Section 304 defines snatching as sudden or forcible seizure/attempt to seize movable property from a person or their possession. Sections 305–307 provide aggravated/theft-related forms and punishment.

Illustration: Example: secretly removing a phone from a table is theft; suddenly grabbing a phone from a person may be snatching.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q2. Define extortion, robbery and dacoity.

Answer framework: Section 308 extortion: intentionally putting a person in fear of injury and thereby dishonestly inducing delivery of property/valuable security. Section 309 robbery: theft or extortion elevated by violence/fear of instant harm in the statutory circumstances. Section 310 dacoity: robbery by five or more persons conjointly or present and aiding; ss.311–312 address aggravated forms/attempts. Section 313 concerns specified gangs.

Illustration: Robbery is not simply a high-value theft; the violence/instant fear element is essential.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q3. Define misappropriation, breach of trust, receiving stolen property.

Answer framework: Sections 314–315 address dishonest misappropriation and related property misapplication; s.316 criminal breach of trust requires entrustment or dominion over property and dishonest misappropriation/conversion/use/disposal contrary to law or contract; s.317 receiving/retaining stolen property requires knowledge or reason to believe it is stolen.

Illustration: Entrustment distinguishes breach of trust from ordinary misappropriation.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q4. Define cheating.

Answer framework: Section 318: deception and dishonest/fraudulent inducement to deliver property, consent to retention, or do/omit an act causing or likely to cause harm as defined. Section 319 addresses cheating by personation. Dishonest intention must ordinarily exist at the time of inducement; a later breach alone does not automatically prove cheating.

Illustration: Example: taking advance money with a false identity and no intention to supply may be cheating; genuine contract followed by inability to perform may be civil breach.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q5. Define intimidation, insult, annoyance and defamation.

Answer framework: Sections 351–356 address criminal intimidation, intentional insult with intent/knowledge to provoke breach of peace, statements/conduct causing public mischief/annoyance in specified circumstances, and defamation. Defamation requires making/publishing an imputation concerning a person with intent/knowledge/reason to believe it will harm reputation, subject to statutory exceptions. Examine truth/public good, good-faith opinion and other exceptions carefully.

Illustration: Example: a threat to injure a person or property to cause alarm may be criminal intimidation; a defamatory imputation must be assessed with its exceptions.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.
