Finding: applicable syllabus

Admission batch: 2024–25 (established in the study-project context). KSLU Circular KSLU/Aca/BOS/2024-25/1586 dated 26 September 2024 introduced the three new criminal laws from academic year 2024–25 and replaced Criminal Law I: IPC with Criminal Law I: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. The BNS came into force on 1 July 2024.

Result: BNS is the prescribed substantive criminal law for this paper for this batch. BNSS and BSA are separate papers/frameworks, not substitutes to be taught as the core Criminal Law–I statute.

Transition caution: For conduct before 1 July 2024, the applicable law may remain IPC under the constitutional non-retroactivity rule and repeal/savings provisions. Old IPC question papers remain useful for concepts, but use BNS sections for BNS-era offences. Do not mix numbers.

Existing project audit

  • study-dashboard.html: useful navigation, study method and explicit warning that uploaded papers include IPC-era questions; unit map is provisional and requires correction.
  • index(20261027-173815).html: substantial BNS theory/Q&A page, five units, expandable long answers and revision progress. It is not a full syllabus textbook; several syllabus clusters are absent or only represented by short Q&A.
  • problem-questions.html: useful IRAC-style problem practice, but answers often use generic application placeholders rather than fully worked statutory analysis. It needs more fact-specific worked problems.
  • KSLU_Criminal_Law_I_BNS_Master_Question_List_8_Week_Plan_WORKING.docx: provisional working map. It incorrectly places public health/religion and some offences in Unit V and over-expands Unit V. The KSLU Appendix-I unit division places public nuisance, mischief/trespass, forgery/property marks in Unit IV; Unit V begins with theft and related property offences and ends with defamation/intimidation etc.
  • 25CriminalLawI.pdf: historical/past paper material. Questions must be dated and labelled IPC or BNS; a paper is not proof of the applicable statute for every year.
Specific statutory commencement trap: BNS s.106(2) (special hit-and-run clause) was not brought into force on 1 July 2024 and is reported still not in force as at 26 October 2026. Section 106(1) is in force. Treat the syllabus reference to hit-and-run as a topic for explanation, not authority to quote the dormant subsection as currently enforceable.

Source hierarchy and limits

Primary syllabus text reproduced in the 3-Year LL.B. syllabus PDF at Appendix-I, pp. 22–23, and the 26 September 2024 circular are the controlling syllabus sources. The available copy is hosted by an affiliated law college; the circular text is also mirrored online. For statutory wording, use India Code / Gazette BNS text and the latest amendments/notifications. Case law is precedent and interpretation, not a substitute for statutory ingredients.

Not all judgments have been exhaustively researched for every offence. The case-law master list prioritises established Supreme Court authorities and labels the IPC provision under which older cases were decided.
