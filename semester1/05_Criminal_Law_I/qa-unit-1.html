Answer practice is organised by marks. For 2 marks give a definition and section; for 5 marks explain the essentials; for 10/15 marks add statutory framework, ingredients, exceptions, cases, examples and conclusion.

2-mark definitions

Q1. Define nature and conception of crime.

Answer framework: A crime is conduct prohibited by law and made punishable by the State. It is a public wrong: prosecution is ordinarily brought in the name of the State. Distinguish crime from tort (private civil remedy), breach of contract (failure to perform a consensual obligation), and moral wrong (social disapproval without necessarily being unlawful). The same act may create both criminal and civil liability.

Illustration: Example: A deliberately assaults B. A may face prosecution and also pay civil compensation.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q2. Define actus reus, mens rea and causation.

Answer framework: Actus reus is the external element: conduct, legally relevant omission, circumstance or consequence. Mens rea is the mental element required by the offence—intention, knowledge, dishonesty, fraudulence or negligence as specified. The prosecution must prove every ingredient beyond reasonable doubt. Causation connects conduct to prohibited consequence. Not every offence requires the same mental state.

Illustration: Example: causing death by a deliberate knife attack differs from an accidental death during a lawful activity.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q3. Define maxims and stages.

Answer framework: Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea; ignorantia facti excusat, ignorantia juris non excusat; volunti non fit injuria is not a universal criminal defence. Stages: intention, preparation, attempt, commission. Mere intention is generally not punishable; preparation is usually not punishable unless specifically criminalised; attempt is punishable under s.62 where applicable.

Illustration: Example: buying a tool may be preparation; using it to break a lock and enter may cross into attempt or completed offence depending on facts.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q4. Define parties and participation.

Answer framework: Principal offender; abettor (ss.45–60 in Unit II); conspirator (s.61); person acting with common intention or common object where relevant provisions apply; aiders and participants. Liability depends on the statutory mode of participation and proof of intention/knowledge. Do not treat presence alone as automatic guilt.

Illustration: Example: a lookout who intentionally facilitates a planned robbery may incur liability if the legal ingredients are proved.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q5. Define bns scope and definitions.

Answer framework: BNS is Act 45 of 2023, in force from 1 July 2024. Section 1 deals with title, commencement and application; s.2 definitions; s.3 general explanations. It replaces IPC for offences committed after commencement, subject to constitutional non-retroactivity and repeal/savings rules. Section 358 repeals IPC while preserving prior liabilities/proceedings as provided.

Illustration: Temporal rule: identify date of conduct first; do not apply BNS retrospectively to an earlier offence where it would impose greater liability.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q6. Write a short note on punishments and sentencing.

Answer framework: Section 4 lists death, imprisonment for life, imprisonment (rigorous/simple), forfeiture of property, fine and community service. Sections 5–13 address commutation, fractions, rigorous/simple form, fine and default, limits for multiple offences, uncertain conviction among offences, solitary confinement and enhanced punishment after previous conviction. Solitary confinement is regulated, not an unlimited free-standing punishment. Sentencing must follow the offence provision, statutory limits and constitutional safeguards.

Illustration: Life imprisonment ordinarily means the remainder of natural life unless lawfully remitted/commuted; it does not automatically mean 14 years.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q7. Write a short note on general exceptions.

Answer framework: Sections 14–44 include mistake of fact in specified good-faith circumstances; judicial acts and court orders; accident; necessity; infancy; unsoundness of mind; involuntary intoxication; consent-related exceptions; good-faith acts; compulsion; slight harm; and private defence. Each exception has conditions and exclusions. The accused bears the applicable evidential burden; prosecution retains the ultimate burden to prove guilt.

Illustration: Example: private defence can justify necessary defensive force, but not revenge after danger ends.
