How to answer a problem question

Read the facts, identify the legal issue, state the rule, apply it to the facts and give a conclusion. Use this easy pattern:

I-R-A-C: Issue → Rule / BNS section → Application → Conclusion.

Model answers below use simple English. Learn the method, not just the wording. In the exam, write the exact statutory section and ingredients from your current Bare Act.

Study note: These are syllabus-based practice scenarios, not all verified past-paper questions. Some conclusions depend on evidence and exact statutory conditions. Check the current BNS text before memorising section details.

PROBLEM 1 · PRACTICE

Mistake of fact

Facts: A police officer arrests a person believing, after checking an official record, that a valid warrant exists. It later turns out that the record was wrong. Can the officer automatically be held criminally liable?

1. Issue

The main issue is: mistake of fact.

2. Rule / applicable law

The main issue is whether the officer acted under a genuine mistake of fact while exercising a legal duty. The BNS general exceptions include protection for certain acts done in good faith under a mistake of fact, but not a mistake of law. The court would examine the officer’s authority, the information checked, good faith and whether the act stayed within the law.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

A genuine and reasonable mistake of fact may provide a defence if all statutory conditions are met. It is not an automatic defence; the facts and the applicable BNS exception must be proved.

PROBLEM 2 · PRACTICE

Private defence

Facts: A attacks B with a stick. B pushes A away to stop the attack. After A falls and the danger ends, B continues beating A and causes serious injuries. Is all of B’s conduct protected by private defence?

1. Issue

The main issue is: private defence.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is the right of private defence and its limits. The BNS recognises private defence of the body and, in defined circumstances, property. The force used must remain within the limits allowed by law and must relate to the threat faced.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

B may be justified in using necessary force to stop the attack. Continuing to beat A after the danger has ended may exceed the right of private defence. Liability depends on the injury, circumstances and statutory limits.

PROBLEM 3 · PRACTICE

Intention and knowledge

Facts: A throws a heavy stone at B’s head from close range. A says he only wanted to frighten B, but B dies. What should the court consider?

1. Issue

The main issue is: intention and knowledge.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is the mental element: intention or knowledge. The court considers the weapon, the part of the body targeted, the force used, distance, prior threats and surrounding circumstances. Under the BNS, culpable homicide and murder are addressed in Sections 100 and 101, with punishment provisions including Section 105 for culpable homicide not amounting to murder.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

A’s statement alone does not decide the case. The court must infer intention or knowledge from all the facts and then decide whether the ingredients of murder or culpable homicide are established, including whether any exception applies.

Abetment by instigation

Facts: A repeatedly urges B to burn C’s shop. B follows the advice and burns it. What possible liability does A face?

1. Issue

The main issue is: abetment by instigation.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is abetment. Section 45 BNS covers abetment through specified forms, including instigation, conspiracy accompanied by an act or illegal omission as required by law, or intentional aid. The prosecution must prove the relevant statutory ingredients and the connection between A’s conduct and the offence.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

If A intentionally instigated B and the legal requirements are proved, A may be liable for abetment. The exact punishment depends on the offence abetted and the applicable BNS provision.

Preparation or attempt

Facts: A buys petrol and matches intending to burn a warehouse. Police arrest A before A reaches the warehouse or takes any step to ignite anything. Is this necessarily an attempt?

1. Issue

The main issue is: preparation or attempt.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is the difference between preparation and attempt. Preparation generally consists of arranging the means. Attempt requires conduct that has moved beyond mere preparation and sufficiently progressed toward committing the offence. Section 62 BNS deals with punishment for attempting offences where its conditions apply.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

On these facts, buying materials alone is likely to be treated as preparation, not necessarily an attempt. The conclusion may change if A takes a direct step toward setting the warehouse on fire.

Agreement to commit an offence

Facts: A and B agree to rob a bank next week. They begin planning the route but are arrested before the robbery. Can conspiracy arise even though the robbery did not occur?

1. Issue

The main issue is: agreement to commit an offence.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is criminal conspiracy under Section 61 BNS. The prosecution must establish an agreement between the persons to do an illegal act, or a lawful act by illegal means. Depending on the object of the agreement, further action may or may not be required by the statutory definition.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

The absence of a completed robbery does not by itself rule out conspiracy. If the required agreement and other legal ingredients are proved, A and B may be liable for criminal conspiracy.

Attempt to murder

The main issue is: attempt to murder.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is whether A’s conduct amounts to an attempt to murder. The court considers intention or knowledge, the act done, the weapon, target and circumstances. Section 109 BNS concerns attempt to murder. Actual injury or death is not always necessary for an attempt offence.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

If the prosecution proves the required intention or knowledge and a sufficient act toward killing B, A may be charged with attempt to murder even though the shot missed. The final result depends on the evidence.

Murder or culpable homicide

Facts: During a sudden argument, A picks up a knife and stabs B in the chest. B dies. A claims that the incident happened in the heat of the moment. How should the offence be analysed?

Sections 100 and 101 BNS define culpable homicide and murder. The court examines the nature of the act, intention or knowledge, the likely consequences, and whether any statutory exception to murder applies. A sudden argument alone does not automatically reduce murder to culpable homicide.

4. Conclusion

The facts may support murder, but the court must assess the evidence and any relevant exception. State the ingredients, apply each important fact and reach a qualified conclusion rather than assuming that every sudden fight is the same.

Unlawful assembly and common object

Facts: Five people gather outside a shop. One member suddenly attacks the shopkeeper for a personal reason that the others did not share. Are all five automatically liable for that attack?

1. Issue

The main issue is: unlawful assembly and common object.

2. Rule / applicable law

The issue is unlawful assembly and liability for acts connected with its common object. Section 189 BNS addresses unlawful assembly. The court must examine the statutory requirements for the assembly, its common object, each person’s membership and the rules governing liability for offences committed by members.

3. Application to the facts

Apply each legal requirement to the facts given in the question. Do not merely copy the definition; explain which facts support or do not support each ingredient.

4. Conclusion

All five are not automatically liable merely because they were present. Liability depends on proof of the statutory elements and whether the attack falls within the applicable common-object rules.

Last-minute checklist

  • Do not write IPC section numbers for a BNS question unless the question specifically asks for historical comparison.
  • Write the facts that support each legal ingredient.
  • Where facts are incomplete, say what additional fact the court must decide.
  • Do not assume intention only from the accused’s explanation; infer it from all relevant circumstances.
  • Use case law only where you know the correct case and principle.
