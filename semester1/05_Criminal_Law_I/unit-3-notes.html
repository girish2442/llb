Official unit scope: Culpable homicide, mob lynching, murder, hit-and-run, causing death by rash/negligent act, suicide-related offences, organised crime, terrorist act; hurt/grievous hurt including permanent disability/vegetative state; wrongful restraint/confinement; force/criminal force/assault; kidnapping, abduction, slavery and forced labour.

Study each topic through definition → ingredients → exceptions/defences → punishment → example → case principle. Check the exact current wording in the Bare Act before reproducing a section verbatim.

Culpable homicide and murder

Section 100 defines culpable homicide: causing death with intention to cause death, intention to cause bodily injury likely to cause death, or knowledge that the act is likely to cause death. Section 101 defines murder through higher degrees of intention/knowledge, subject to five statutory exceptions. Section 103 provides punishment for murder; s.105 for culpable homicide not amounting to murder. Mob lynching is addressed within the murder/culpable homicide provisions identified in the syllabus.

Simple example: Use the three-stage approach in Rayavarapu Punnayya: causation, culpable homicide, then whether murder or an exception applies.

Death by negligence, hit and run; suicide

Section 106 addresses causing death by negligence, with specific sub-rules including the hit-and-run context; check the latest operative text and commencement notifications before quoting any proviso. Sections 107–108 deal with abetment of suicide in specified victim categories. Section 109 is attempt to murder. Distinguish negligence (lack of due care) from intention/knowledge required for homicide.

Simple example: A road accident is not automatically criminal negligence: prove the statutory degree of rashness/negligence and causation.

Organised crime and terrorist act

Sections 111–112 address organised crime and petty organised crime; s.113 terrorist act. These are specially defined offences requiring statutory elements such as continuing unlawful activity, syndicate/organised group, specified serious harm or terror purpose, and prescribed thresholds. Check overlap with special laws and statutory exclusions.

Simple example: A group crime is not automatically “organised crime”; prove the special statutory definition.

Hurt and grievous hurt

Section 114 defines hurt; s.116 lists grievous hurt categories; ss.115 and 117 deal with voluntarily causing hurt/grievous hurt; ss.118–125 cover aggravated forms including dangerous weapons, acid attacks, endangering life, hurt to extort/confess, deter public servant, etc. Identify bodily pain, disease or infirmity; for grievous hurt match one of the exhaustive statutory categories.

Simple example: Example: fracture is grievous hurt; a minor bruise is ordinarily simple hurt.

Restraint, confinement, force and assault

Sections 126–127: wrongful restraint obstructs a person from proceeding where they have a right to proceed; wrongful confinement unlawfully restrains within circumscribed limits. Sections 128–136 define force, criminal force and assault and provide offences/aggravations. Criminal force requires intentional use of force without consent to cause specified harm/fear/annoyance; assault involves gesture/preparation causing apprehension of imminent criminal force.

Simple example: Blocking a road path may be restraint; locking someone in a room is confinement.

Kidnapping, abduction, slavery and forced labour

Sections 137–146 address kidnapping, abduction, kidnapping for begging, kidnapping/maiming a child, trafficking, exploitation, slavery and unlawful compulsory labour. Distinguish kidnapping (taking/enticing from lawful guardianship or from India as defined) from abduction (force or deceitful means to induce movement). Abduction is often an ingredient of a further offence.

Simple example: Consent of a child does not necessarily defeat kidnapping from lawful guardianship.

Exam writing checklist

  1. Start with the legal concept and correct BNS section.
  2. Set out each ingredient separately.
  3. Explain exceptions, provisos and statutory explanations.
  4. Apply the facts or give a short illustration.
  5. State punishment only after checking the exact subsection.
  6. Use a case for its principle and identify if it was decided under IPC.
