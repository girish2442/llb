Answer practice is organised by marks. For 2 marks give a definition and section; for 5 marks explain the essentials; for 10/15 marks add statutory framework, ingredients, exceptions, cases, examples and conclusion.

2-mark definitions

Q1. Define culpable homicide and murder.

Answer framework: Section 100 defines culpable homicide: causing death with intention to cause death, intention to cause bodily injury likely to cause death, or knowledge that the act is likely to cause death. Section 101 defines murder through higher degrees of intention/knowledge, subject to five statutory exceptions. Section 103 provides punishment for murder; s.105 for culpable homicide not amounting to murder. Mob lynching is addressed within the murder/culpable homicide provisions identified in the syllabus.

Illustration: Use the three-stage approach in Rayavarapu Punnayya: causation, culpable homicide, then whether murder or an exception applies.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q2. Define death by negligence, hit and run; suicide.

Answer framework: Section 106 addresses causing death by negligence, with specific sub-rules including the hit-and-run context; check the latest operative text and commencement notifications before quoting any proviso. Sections 107–108 deal with abetment of suicide in specified victim categories. Section 109 is attempt to murder. Distinguish negligence (lack of due care) from intention/knowledge required for homicide.

Illustration: A road accident is not automatically criminal negligence: prove the statutory degree of rashness/negligence and causation.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q3. Define organised crime and terrorist act.

Answer framework: Sections 111–112 address organised crime and petty organised crime; s.113 terrorist act. These are specially defined offences requiring statutory elements such as continuing unlawful activity, syndicate/organised group, specified serious harm or terror purpose, and prescribed thresholds. Check overlap with special laws and statutory exclusions.

Illustration: A group crime is not automatically “organised crime”; prove the special statutory definition.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q4. Define hurt and grievous hurt.

Answer framework: Section 114 defines hurt; s.116 lists grievous hurt categories; ss.115 and 117 deal with voluntarily causing hurt/grievous hurt; ss.118–125 cover aggravated forms including dangerous weapons, acid attacks, endangering life, hurt to extort/confess, deter public servant, etc. Identify bodily pain, disease or infirmity; for grievous hurt match one of the exhaustive statutory categories.

Illustration: Example: fracture is grievous hurt; a minor bruise is ordinarily simple hurt.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q5. Define restraint, confinement, force and assault.

Answer framework: Sections 126–127: wrongful restraint obstructs a person from proceeding where they have a right to proceed; wrongful confinement unlawfully restrains within circumscribed limits. Sections 128–136 define force, criminal force and assault and provide offences/aggravations. Criminal force requires intentional use of force without consent to cause specified harm/fear/annoyance; assault involves gesture/preparation causing apprehension of imminent criminal force.

Illustration: Blocking a road path may be restraint; locking someone in a room is confinement.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.
