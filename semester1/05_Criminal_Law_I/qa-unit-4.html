Answer practice is organised by marks. For 2 marks give a definition and section; for 5 marks explain the essentials; for 10/15 marks add statutory framework, ingredients, exceptions, cases, examples and conclusion.

2-mark definitions

Q1. Define offences against the state.

Answer framework: Sections 147–158 include waging/attempting/abetting war against Government of India, conspiracy, collecting arms, concealing design, assaulting high constitutional authorities and acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity. Section 152 addresses acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India. Distinguish violent war/insurrection offences from speech-related conduct; read the exact statutory language and constitutional protections under Article 19.

Illustration: Example: an organised armed attack aimed at overthrowing the government is qualitatively different from peaceful criticism.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q2. Define election offences.

Answer framework: Sections 168–174 include candidate/public servant election-related offences, bribery, undue influence, personation and false statements, subject to the definitions and exceptions. The remaining syllabus range ss.175–188 covers coin, currency notes, bank-notes and government stamps. Identify the protected electoral process or instrument and the precise prohibited conduct.

Illustration: Example: offering a voter money to influence their vote may amount to electoral bribery.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q3. Define public tranquillity.

Answer framework: Sections 189–197 cover unlawful assembly, common object liability, rioting, liability of members, affray, promoting enmity between groups and assertions prejudicial to national integration. Unlawful assembly requires five or more persons and a statutorily prohibited common object; rioting requires force/violence by an unlawful assembly or member in prosecution of common object.

Illustration: Distinguish unlawful assembly from mere gathering; common object and statutory purpose matter.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q4. Define public justice and evidence.

Answer framework: Sections 227–238 cover giving/fabricating false evidence, punishment for false evidence, threats to induce false evidence, screening offender, destruction of evidence, false information and harbouring offender. Fabrication involves creating circumstances/false entries intended to cause an authority to form an erroneous opinion on a material point.

Illustration: Example: knowingly creating a false document for use in court may amount to fabrication/forgery depending on ingredients.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.

Q5. Define public nuisance.

Answer framework: Sections 270–273 address public nuisance and related consequences/continuance. Public nuisance affects the public or a class of people, not merely one private individual. Compare with private nuisance, which is a tort concerning interference with use/enjoyment of land.

Illustration: Example: blocking a public street in a way that affects the community may constitute public nuisance.

Exam finish: State the applicable BNS section and punishment only after checking the precise subsection in the current Bare Act.
