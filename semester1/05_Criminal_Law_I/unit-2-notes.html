Official unit scope: Abetment (ss.45–60), abetment of offence by person outside India; conspiracy (s.61); attempt (s.62); rape and other sexual offences/deceitful means (ss.63–73); criminal force/assault against women (ss.74–79); dowry death (s.80), bigamy (s.82), cruelty (ss.85–86), kidnapping (s.87); offences against children (ss.91–97).

Statutory map: BNS ss.45–99; syllabus-selected provisions ss.45–62, 63–73, 74–79, 80, 82, 85–87, 91–97.

Study each topic through definition → ingredients → exceptions/defences → punishment → example → case principle. Check the exact current wording in the Bare Act before reproducing a section verbatim.

Abetment

Section 45 recognises abetment by instigation, conspiracy accompanied by an act/illegal omission in pursuance, or intentional aid. Sections 46–60 address abettor, liability, consequences, abetment of offences outside India and related situations. Prove intentional participation and the relevant statutory connection; mere advice or presence is not automatically abetment.

Simple example: Example: deliberately persuading another to commit arson, with the required intent, may be instigation.

Conspiracy and attempt

Section 61 criminalises an agreement to do an illegal act or a legal act by illegal means; statutory rules distinguish conspiracies and required overt acts. Section 62 punishes attempt where no specific attempt punishment is provided. Attempt requires an act sufficiently proximate to commission; preparation alone usually is insufficient.

Simple example: Distinguish: abetment by conspiracy is a mode of abetment under s.45; criminal conspiracy is a distinct offence under s.61.

Rape and related sexual offences

Sections 63–73 cover rape definition and punishment, aggravated forms, gang rape, repeat offenders, disclosure of identity and related sexual offences including intercourse by deceitful means under the specific provision. Apply the statutory definition, age, consent, force/coercion, incapacity and exceptions exactly. Consent means an unequivocal voluntary agreement; absence of physical resistance alone is not consent. Read each punishment subsection separately.

Simple example: Do not import the IPC marital exception or section numbering without checking BNS text and constitutional decisions.

Assault and criminal force against women

Sections 74–79 cover assault/criminal force with intent to outrage modesty, sexual harassment, disrobing, voyeurism, stalking and words/gestures intended to insult modesty. Ingredients vary: conduct, intention/knowledge, repetition (for stalking), privacy and statutory exceptions.

Simple example: Example: repeated unwanted following despite clear disinterest may satisfy stalking ingredients if statutory conditions are met.

Marriage-related offences

Section 80 dowry death: death of a woman by burns/bodily injury or otherwise than normal circumstances within seven years of marriage, with cruelty/harassment soon before death in connection with dowry demand; statutory presumption under evidence law may arise when foundational facts proved. Section 82 bigamy: marrying again during subsistence of a valid marriage, subject to statutory exceptions. Sections 85–86 cruelty: wilful conduct of specified grave nature or harassment to coerce unlawful demand; explain statutory definition and proof.

Simple example: Example: persistent dowry-linked harassment shortly before an unnatural death within seven years may raise dowry-death liability if all elements are proved.

Kidnapping and child offences

Section 87 covers kidnapping/abduction of woman with intent to compel marriage or illicit intercourse. Sections 91–97 deal with specified offences involving children, including concealment/abandonment, procuration, selling/buying child for prostitution and other prescribed exploitation. Read exact age thresholds and intent requirements from each section.

Simple example: Example: taking a minor from lawful guardianship without consent may constitute kidnapping regardless of the minor’s apparent willingness, subject to statutory definition.

Exam writing checklist

  1. Start with the legal concept and correct BNS section.
  2. Set out each ingredient separately.
  3. Explain exceptions, provisos and statutory explanations.
  4. Apply the facts or give a short illustration.
  5. State punishment only after checking the exact subsection.
  6. Use a case for its principle and identify if it was decided under IPC.
