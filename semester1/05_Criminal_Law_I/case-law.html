New-law judicial developments: For BNS s.152, see the Rajasthan High Court decision in Tejender Pal Singh v. State of Rajasthan (2024 LiveLaw (Raj) 413) cautioning against using the provision to suppress legitimate dissent, and Mohammed Zubair v. State of Uttar Pradesh (Allahabad High Court, 21 May 2025) for a developing interpretation. These are developing High Court authorities, not a final Supreme Court construction of s.152.

Many foundational cases were decided under the IPC. Their interpretive principles remain useful where the BNS wording is materially continuous; always identify the original IPC provision and verify any BNS textual change before relying on the case.

CaseFacts (brief)PrincipleRelevance / statutory note
State of A.P. v. Rayavarapu Punnayya (1976) 4 SCC 382A group assault caused fatal injuries; issue was culpable homicide versus murder.Culpable homicide is the genus and murder its species; use the structured three-stage analysis.Unit III; IPC ss.299–300; apply to BNS ss.100–101 only insofar as language remains materially consistent.
Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab, AIR 1958 SC 465A deliberate injury caused death; the Court analysed the third clause of murder.For the clause dealing with bodily injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death, prove the injury, its nature, intention to inflict that particular injury and objective sufficiency.Unit III; IPC s.300 thirdly; relevant to BNS murder analysis.
K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra, AIR 1962 SC 605Accused pleaded grave and sudden provocation after discovering his wife’s relationship.Explained the test for grave and sudden provocation and loss of self-control; exception is fact-sensitive.Unit III; murder exception; IPC-era authority.
Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980) 2 SCC 684Constitutionality of death penalty was challenged.Death penalty constitutionally valid but limited to the rarest of rare cases after considering aggravating and mitigating circumstances.Unit I and III; sentencing principle.
Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab (1983) 3 SCC 470Supreme Court elaborated categories for rarest-of-rare sentencing.Guidance for applying Bachan Singh; not a mechanical checklist.Unit I and III; sentencing.
Dahyabhai Chhaganbhai Thakkar v. State of Gujarat, AIR 1964 SC 1563Accused raised insanity defence.Explained evidential burden for an exception and how prosecution burden interacts with defence evidence.Unit I; unsoundness of mind.
Basdev v. State of Pepsu, AIR 1956 SC 488Accused relied on intoxication in a homicide case.Voluntary intoxication does not automatically excuse; assess whether required intention/knowledge was formed, subject to statutory rules.Unit I; intoxication.
Darshan Singh v. State of Punjab (2010) 2 SCC 333Supreme Court considered private defence.Private defence is preventive, not retaliatory; reasonable apprehension and proportionality are central, not mathematical equality of force.Unit I; BNS ss.34–44.
Kehar Singh v. State (Delhi Administration) (1988) 3 SCC 609Alleged conspiracy in assassination case.Conspiracy is usually proved by circumstances; agreement is the core ingredient.Unit II; BNS s.61.
State of Maharashtra v. Mohd. Yakub (1980) 3 SCC 57Accused were intercepted during export of silver; issue was attempt.Attempt begins when preparation moves into an act sufficiently proximate to commission; test is contextual.Unit II; BNS s.62.
State of Punjab v. Gurmit Singh (1996) 2 SCC 384Rape conviction based on victim testimony.Victim testimony can sustain conviction if reliable; corroboration is not an inflexible rule.Unit II; sexual offences.
Independent Thought v. Union of India (2017) 10 SCC 800Challenge to marital rape exception for wives aged 15–18.Read down IPC exception to protect minor wives; statutory child-protection principle.Unit II; read with current BNS text and later legal developments; do not overstate as a blanket adult marital-rape ruling.
Satvir Singh v. State of Punjab (2001) 8 SCC 633Dowry death and dowry demand considered.Dowry death requires proof of statutory ingredients including dowry-linked cruelty/harassment soon before death.Unit II; BNS s.80.
Kaliyaperumal v. State of Tamil Nadu (2004) 9 SCC 157Supreme Court analysed dowry-death ingredients.Explained foundational facts for dowry death and evidentiary presumption.Unit II; BNS s.80.
Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014) 8 SCC 273Arrest in dowry/cruelty case challenged.Arrest is not automatic; police and magistrates must follow statutory safeguards.Unit II; procedural context, not BNS offence definition.
Lalita Kumari v. Government of U.P. (2014) 2 SCC 1Police refusal to register FIR considered.Registration of FIR mandatory where information discloses cognizable offence, subject to limited preliminary inquiry categories.Cross-reference only; procedure now governed by BNSS, not Criminal Law I.
Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab (2005) 6 SCC 1Criminal negligence in medical treatment considered.Criminal negligence requires a high degree of negligence; civil negligence alone is insufficient.Unit III; BNS s.106 principle.
State of Karnataka v. Satish (1998) 8 SCC 493Road accident prosecution for rash driving.Rashness/negligence cannot be presumed merely from speed; facts must establish culpable conduct.Unit III; BNS s.106.
B.P. Radha Krishna v. State of Karnataka (2000) 1 SCC 679Supreme Court discussed common intention principles.Common intention may develop on the spot; participation and shared intention must be inferred from facts.Unit III / participation.
Masalti v. State of U.P., AIR 1965 SC 202Large group attack and unlawful assembly liability.Common object can be inferred from conduct and circumstances; membership and statutory conditions must be proved.Unit IV; BNS s.189 onwards.
Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar, AIR 1962 SC 955Constitutional validity of sedition provision considered.Restricted application to incitement to violence/public disorder; IPC s.124A is repealed and BNS s.152 is distinct.Unit IV; historical constitutional context only, not a direct substitute for s.152 interpretation.
Bilal Ahmed Kaloo v. State of A.P. (1997) 7 SCC 431Conviction for promoting enmity considered.Explained ingredients and need to establish promotion of enmity between groups.Unit IV; BNS s.196.
M. Krishna Reddy v. State Deputy Superintendent of Police (1992) 4 SCC 45False evidence and proof requirements considered.False evidence requires intentional falsity on a material point; distinguish mistake from deliberate fabrication.Unit IV; public justice.
Indian Oil Corporation v. NEPC India Ltd. (2006) 6 SCC 736Criminal proceedings arising from commercial dispute.Civil dispute does not bar criminal prosecution if criminal ingredients independently exist; criminal law must not be used merely to pressure debtors.Unit V; cheating/breach of trust.
Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma v. State of Bihar (2000) 4 SCC 168Dispute over contractual performance alleged as cheating.Dishonest intention at inception is central to cheating; later breach alone is insufficient.Unit V; BNS s.318.
R.K. Dalmia v. Delhi Administration, AIR 1962 SC 1821Entrustment and criminal breach of trust considered.Entrustment/dominion and dishonest misappropriation are central; property may be broadly understood.Unit V; BNS s.316.
Pyare Lal Bhargava v. State of Rajasthan, AIR 1963 SC 1094Government file temporarily removed and returned.Temporary removal can amount to dishonest misappropriation depending on intention and statutory ingredients.Unit V; property offences.
K.N. Mehra v. State of Rajasthan, AIR 1957 SC 369Aircraft taken without consent; theft intention considered.Dishonest intention may be inferred from unauthorised taking and circumstances.Unit V; BNS s.303.
Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India (2016) 7 SCC 221Constitutional challenge to criminal defamation.Criminal defamation upheld; reputation is protected under Article 21, balanced with free speech.Unit V; BNS s.356.
Manik Taneja v. State of Karnataka (2015) 7 SCC 423Online comments alleged to constitute criminal intimidation.Threat and intention to cause alarm are essential; mere criticism does not automatically amount to intimidation.Unit V; BNS s.351.
