PART 1 — Meaning, Definitions & Tortious Liability

1. What is a Tort? ⚪ syllabus foundation — always the opening line of any answer

CORE IDEATort = a civil wrong, independent of contract, remedied usually by unliquidated damages. From Latin "tortum" = twisted/crooked — conduct that is "twisted" away from lawful conduct. Formula: Wrongful Act/Omission + Legal Damage + Legal Remedy → Tortious Liability.

2. Definitions — Winfield, Salmond, Fraser 🟠 definition/theory theme — recurring

WINFIELD
tap to flip
Tortious liability arises from breach of a duty primarily fixed by law; the duty is owed to persons generally, and its breach is redressable by an action for unliquidated damages.
SALMOND
tap to flip
A civil wrong for which the remedy is a common law action for unliquidated damages, and which is not exclusively the breach of a contract, trust or other equitable obligation.
FRASER ⚪ syllabus
tap to flip
Tort is an infringement of a right in rem (a right available against the world at large) of a private individual, giving rise to a right of compensation at the suit of the injured party.
🎯 EXAM ANGLE — Possible question: "Explain/compare the definitions of Tort given by Winfield and Salmond." State both in full, then one line each on what they emphasise: Winfield = duty fixed by law (broad); Salmond = specific civil wrong excluding contract/trust (narrower).

3. Tortious Liability ➕ ADDED — was missing 🔴 Asked in: 2016, 2024

MEANING"Tortious liability" is the legal responsibility that arises specifically from a tort — as opposed to liability arising from a contract (voluntarily agreed) or a crime (a public wrong punished by the State). It arises the moment the three essentials (wrongful act + legal damage + legal remedy) are satisfied, independent of any agreement between the parties.

4. Essential Elements — remember W + D + R 🔴 core of almost every Unit I answer

  • Wrongful Act/Omission — breach of a legal duty, not merely a moral one (e.g. reckless driving; a doctor's failure to warn of a known risk)
  • Legal Damage (Injuria) — violation of a legal right; actual loss is not always needed (see Injuria/Damnum below)
  • Legal Remedy — generally unliquidated (court-assessed) damages
Case: Ashby v White (1703) 🧩 added case-box
Facts: A qualified voter was wrongfully denied his vote by the returning officer; his preferred candidate still won, so no financial loss occurred.
Decision: Held liable — damages awarded.
Principle: Violation of a legal right is itself actionable, even without actual loss — this single case supports THREE topics at once: essential elements, injuria sine damno, and Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium.
🧩 THINK & ANSWER: A person's phone is stolen but he later gets it back undamaged and unused. Has a tort been committed? (Yes — the legal right to possession was violated the moment it was taken; return of the phone doesn't erase the tortious act, though it may reduce damages.)

ONE-LINE MEMORYWINFIELD = wider/general principle → "Law of Tort". SALMOND = narrower/specific list → "Law of Torts".

6. Development / Evolution of Law of Torts 🟠 Asked in: 2011, 2012, 2023 — keep this brief

Evolution — quick flow (tap)
English common law/writ system → 18th–19th c. case-law expansion (esp. negligence, Industrial Revolution) → adopted in India via colonial courts (justice, equity, good conscience) → post-Independence Indian courts developed State liability & constitutional torts (State of Rajasthan v Vidhyawati; Kasturilal v State of UP; Rudal Shah v State of Bihar; Bhim Singh v State of J&K) → absolute liability (M.C. Mehta v Union of India).

NoteThese State-liability and absolute-liability cases are named here only as one-line evolution markers — their full facts/decision/principle belong to Units II (vicarious/State liability) and III (absolute liability), so don't over-prepare them here.

PART 2 — Civil Wrongs, Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium & Legal Damage

7. "All torts are civil wrongs, but all civil wrongs are not torts" ➕ ADDED — was missing 🔴 Asked in: 2012, 2019, 2020, 2022, 2025

MEANING"Civil wrongs" is the wider umbrella category; "torts" is a narrower category within it. The wider category of civil wrongs also includes breach of contract and breach of trust — neither of which is a tort, because Salmond's own definition specifically excludes wrongs that are "exclusively" a breach of contract or trust.
  • Why every tort is a civil wrong — remedied by civil action, principal remedy = unliquidated damages.
  • Why every civil wrong isn't a tort — e.g. if A breaks a contractual promise to deliver goods, B's remedy is contractual, not tortious, because the duty arose from agreement, not from a duty independently fixed by law.
🎯 EXAM ANGLE — Possible question: "All torts are civil wrongs, but all civil wrongs are not torts." Discuss/justify with cases. Structure: meaning → why every tort is civil → why breach of contract/trust isn't automatically a tort (with the illustration above) → conclusion on source of duty being the key differentiator.

9. Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium 🔴 Asked in: 2011, 2012, 2013, 2019, 2021, 2022, 2024

MEANING"Where there is a right, there is a remedy." Wherever a legally recognised right is violated, the law must provide a means of redress — otherwise the right would be meaningless. Applies only to legal rights, not moral/social/religious ones.
Case: Ashby v White (1703) — reused, this is the maxim's leading case
Facts: Qualified voter wrongfully stopped from voting; his candidate still won — no financial loss.
Decision: Plaintiff succeeded.
Principle: Because a legal right existed, its violation was actionable in itself — the law provided a remedy even without proof of loss.
🎯 EXAM ANGLE — Possible question: "Explain/trace the development of Ubi Jus Ibi Remedium." Meaning → scope (legal rights only) → Ashby v White in full → limitation (doesn't create new rights, only remedies existing ones) → conclusion.

10. Legal Damage 🟠 Asked in: 2021, 2026

MEANINGLegal damage means the infringement of a legal right, distinct from actual/factual loss. "Injuria" = legal injury (right violated); "Damnum" = actual loss. Tort law is concerned with injuria, not merely damnum.
🎯 EXAM ANGLE — Possible question: "Explain legal damage as an essential element of Tort." This is really the same core idea as Injuria/Damnum below — write the definition, then go straight into the two maxims as your supporting material so you aren't repeating yourself across two answers.

11 & 12. Injuria Sine Damno vs Damnum Sine Injuria 🔴 Asked in: 2011, 2012, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2021, 2023, 2026

ALSO ASKED AS"Every injury imports damage but every damage is not injury" (2013, 2020, 2023, 2024) is the same theme in different wording — one answer covers both question forms.
BasisInjuria sine damnoDamnum sine injuria
MeaningLegal injury, no actual lossActual loss, no legal injury
Actionable?YESNO
CaseAshby v White (1703)Gloucester Grammar School Case (1410)
Case: Ashby v White (1703) — full case-box 🧩 added
Facts: A qualified voter was wrongfully and maliciously denied his vote by the returning officer; his candidate won anyway, so no financial loss resulted.
Decision: Defendant held liable, damages awarded.
Principle: Violation of a legal right is actionable by itself, regardless of actual loss — injuria sine damno.
Case: Gloucester Grammar School Case (1410) — full case-box 🧩 added
Facts: The defendant opened a rival school next door, charging lower fees; the plaintiff's school lost students and income.
Decision: No action lay against the defendant.
Principle: Loss caused by lawful competition, without violation of any legal right, is not actionable — damnum sine injuria.
📌 MEMORY: RIGHT violated + LOSS absent = still actionable (injuria sine damno). LOSS present + RIGHT not violated = not actionable (damnum sine injuria).
🎯 EXAM ANGLE — Possible question: "Explain Injuria sine damno and Damnum sine injuria with decided cases" OR "Distinguish Injuria sine damno and Damnum sine injuria." Same material either way — definition of each → full case → the distinction table → one-line conclusion.

PART 3 — Mental Elements in Tort

CORE IDEA🔴 Asked in: 2011, 2013, 2018, 2023, 2024, 2026
Tort law does not require mens rea the way criminal law does — liability can rest on intention, negligence, or strict liability. Still, the mental state matters for specific torts. Four concepts to master: Intention • Motive • Malice in Law • Malice in Fact.
INTENTION
tap to flip
Conscious desire/purpose to bring about an act or its consequence. Relevant in assault, battery, false imprisonment, deceit, malicious prosecution. NOT needed for negligence or strict liability.
MOTIVE
tap to flip
The "why" behind the act. Generally irrelevant — good motive ≠ excuse; bad motive ≠ liability for a lawful act. Exceptions: malicious prosecution, conspiracy, deceit, qualified privilege.
MALICE IN LAW
tap to flip
Doing a wrongful act intentionally, without lawful justification. Presumed from the act itself — personal ill-will need not be proved. E.g. deliberate publication of a false defamatory statement.
MALICE IN FACT
tap to flip
Actual spite, hatred or improper motive against the plaintiff. Must be proved as a fact — e.g. prosecuting someone purely out of personal revenge.

Facts: Pickles diverted underground water on his own land with the bad motive of forcing Bradford Corporation to buy his land at a high price.
Decision: No liability — he was exercising a lawful right.
Principle: A lawful act does not become tortious merely because it is done with a bad motive.
