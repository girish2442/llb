PART 16 — Consumer Protection Act, 1986

WHY THIS ACTBefore CPA 1986, consumers had to go to civil courts — expensive, slow, complex. The Act gives a simpler, speedy, inexpensive redressal mechanism.

Objectives

Protect consumers from exploitation · simple/speedy/inexpensive redressal · promote & protect consumer rights · establish consumer councils & redressal agencies.

Six Consumer Rights

Safety
Protection against goods/services hazardous to life & property
Information
Know quality, quantity, purity, standard and price
Choice
Access to a variety of goods/services at competitive prices
Be Heard
Consumer interests must get due consideration
Redressal
Remedy against unfair/restrictive practices & exploitation
Consumer Education
Knowledge/awareness to make informed choices

Who is a Consumer? — Sec. 2(1)(d)

Core test: BUY/HIRE + CONSIDERATION + permitted purpose = CONSUMER. Consideration can be paid, promised, partly paid/promised, or under deferred payment. Approved users/beneficiaries also included in stated circumstances.
NOT a ConsumerException
Buying goods for resale—
Obtaining goods/services primarily for commercial profitSelf-employment livelihood exception — using goods exclusively to earn a livelihood by self-employment can still qualify (Cheema Engineering Services, 1997)

Defect vs Deficiency

📌 DEFECT = GOODS · DEFICIENCY = SERVICES
Defect in GoodsDeficiency in Services
Fault/imperfection/shortcoming in quality, quantity, purity or standard required/claimed — e.g. underweight, sub-standard, impure/adulterated, not matching claimed specFault/imperfection/shortcoming/inadequacy in quality, nature or manner of performance of a service required by law or undertaken by contract

Medical Services & Government Bodies

Case: Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha (1995)
Principle: Medical services fall within "services" under CPA 1986 — patients who pay for treatment are consumers and can complain of deficiency in service, including medical negligence.
Case: Spring Meadows Hospital
Principle: Used to illustrate medical negligence as deficiency in service.
Case: Lucknow Development Authority v M.K. Gupta (1994)
Principle: Government bodies providing services can be covered under CPA — important authority for bringing certain government service activities within consumer protection.

Three-Tier Redressal Structure

District Forum — up to ₹20 lakh→State Commission — ₹20L to ₹1 crore→National Commission — above ₹1 crore
📌 D → S → N = District → State → National
Procedure
Written complaint → notice to opposite party → hearing → lab testing where needed (for goods) → order → appeal to next level. Disposal: 90 days, or 150 days if lab testing needed.

Remedies under CPA

Removal of defects · Replacement of goods · Refund of price · Compensation for loss/injury · Removal of deficiency in service · Discontinuance of unfair trade practices · Withdrawal of hazardous goods · Costs & other appropriate reliefs.

Limitation & Penalties

Limitation
tap
2 years from the date the cause of action arises. Delay may be condoned for sufficient cause.
Penalty for non-compliance
tap
Imprisonment: 1 month to 3 years, and/or fine ₹2,000 to ₹10,000 (1986 framework figures).
2019 Update — Awareness Only
CPA 1986 → replaced by CPA 2019. New concepts: Product Liability (manufacturer/service provider/seller), CCPA (Central Consumer Protection Authority), E-commerce coverage, Mediation route, expanded E-filing. Study 1986 for this syllabus; know these only as awareness.
📌 PART 16: Consumer = Buy/Hire + Consideration + permitted purpose. Defect=goods, Deficiency=services. D→S→N tiers. 2-year limitation. IMA v Shantha=medical; LDA v Gupta=government bodies.

PART 17 — Motor Vehicles Act, 1988

COREFramework for regulating motor vehicles & protecting accident victims — compulsory third-party insurance, no-fault liability, specialised Claims Tribunals, offence provisions.

Key Sections — flip cards

Sec. 146 — Insurance
tap
Compulsory third-party insurance — vehicles used in public places must carry it, ensuring compensation to injured/killed third parties.
Sec. 140 — No-Fault Liability
tap
Compensation payable for death/permanent disablement WITHOUT proving negligence. Death = ₹50,000; Permanent disablement = ₹25,000. Contributory negligence is NOT a defence here. Additional to other compensation rights.
Sec. 161 — Hit & Run
tap
Where offending vehicle can't be identified — claim from Solatium Fund. Death = ₹25,000; Grievous hurt = ₹12,500.
Sec. 165 — MACT
tap
Motor Accidents Claims Tribunal — exclusive jurisdiction over death, bodily injury, third-party property damage claims from accidents.
📌 146=Insurance · 140=No-Fault (50K death/25K disablement) · 161=Hit&Run (25K/12.5K) · 165=MACT

Section 163A & Pay-and-Recover

Sec. 163A: structured-formula route to compensation without proving negligence under ordinary fault-based tort principles. Pay & Recover: even where the insurer has a statutory defence against the insured, it must first pay the third-party victim, then recover from the insured — protects the innocent victim.

MACT — Who / Procedure / Compensation

Who may apply: accident victim, legal representatives of deceased, authorised agent.
Application→Notice to owner/driver/insurer→Policy & defence→Evidence/inquiry→Award→Payment→Appeal
Multiplier Method & Sarla Verma v DTC (2009)
Method: Annual dependency × age-based multiplier for death claims.
Principle: Sarla Verma v Delhi Transport Corporation (2009) standardised the multiplier method for calculating just compensation. (General Manager, Kerala SRTC v Susamma Thomas (1994) is an earlier case on multiplier/just compensation.)
