Law of Torts — Master Case-Law List

KSLU · 3-Year LL.B. · Semester I · Exam-focused study resource

IndexGap report

Use this as a principle-first case memory aid. Facts are brief identifying summaries, not full case reports. Confirm report citations and current status from prescribed materials before quoting.

CaseEssential factsPrinciple / relevanceUnit
Ashby v White (1703)Returning officer wrongfully denied a qualified voter his vote; no financial loss because preferred candidate won.Violation of a legal right is actionable without proof of actual loss (injuria sine damno).Unit I
Gloucester Grammar School Case (1410)A rival school opened and charged lower fees, causing economic loss.Lawful competition causing loss without violation of a legal right is damnum sine injuria.Unit I
Bradford Corporation v Pickles (1895)Pickles diverted underground water on his own land, allegedly with spiteful motive.A lawful exercise of property rights is not tortious merely because motive is malicious.Unit I
Donoghue v Stevenson (1932)Opaque bottle allegedly contained a decomposed snail; consumer became ill.Neighbour principle; duty of care to persons closely and directly affected.Unit III
Blyth v Birmingham Waterworks (1856)Waterworks apparatus failed during exceptionally severe frost.Negligence assessed by reasonable-person standard in circumstances.Unit III
Barnett v Chelsea & Kensington Hospital (1969)Patient died after hospital negligently failed to examine him; evidence showed death would have occurred anyway.But-for causation not established; negligence must cause the damage.Unit III
The Wagon Mound (No. 1) (1961)Oil spill caught fire and damaged wharf; fire damage was not reasonably foreseeable.Remoteness turns on reasonable foreseeability of the kind of damage.Unit III
Smith v Leech Brain (1962)Minor burn triggered cancer due to claimant’s pre-existing vulnerability.Thin-skull rule: take claimant as found once kind of injury is foreseeable.Unit III
Byrne v Boadle (1863)Barrel fell from warehouse window onto passer-by.Res ipsa loquitur may permit inference of negligence.Unit III
Scott v London & St Katherine Docks (1865)Bags of sugar fell from crane onto plaintiff.Classic formulation of res ipsa conditions.Unit III
Donoghue v Stevenson (1932)Consumer injury from contaminated product.Duty of care / neighbour principle.Unit III
Rylands v Fletcher (1868)Reservoir water escaped through old mine shafts and flooded mine.Strict liability for escape of dangerous thing brought onto land for non-natural use, subject to exceptions.Unit III
Nichols v Marsland (1876)Exceptional rainfall caused artificial lakes to burst.Act of God exception to Rylands strict liability.Unit III
M.C. Mehta v Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak) (1987)Oleum gas escaped from hazardous industrial plant in Delhi.Absolute and non-delegable liability of enterprises engaged in hazardous/inherently dangerous activity.Unit III
Sturges v Bridgman (1879)Confectioner’s machinery disturbed doctor’s consulting rooms.Locality and substantial/unreasonable interference in private nuisance; coming to nuisance no defence.Unit III
Rose v Miles (1815)Obstruction of navigable waterway forced plaintiff to incur extra transport costs.Individual suing for public nuisance must show special damage.Unit III
Hall v Brooklands Auto Racing Club (1933)Spectator injured by car during motor race.Volenti may apply to ordinary inherent risks freely accepted.Unit II
Smith v Baker (1891)Worker knew of danger from crane but continued employment.Knowledge of risk is not necessarily voluntary consent.Unit II
Haynes v Harwood (1935)Police constable injured stopping runaway horses left unattended.Rescue doctrine; volenti generally does not defeat claim by rescuer.Unit II
Davies v Mann (1842)Negligently left animal was run over by defendant’s wagon.Historical last-opportunity rule; modern contributory negligence uses apportionment principles.Unit II
Nichols v Marsland (1876)Unprecedented rainfall overwhelmed artificial lakes.Act of God.Unit II/III
Stanley v Powell (1891)Shot glanced off tree during properly conducted shooting.Inevitable accident despite reasonable care.Unit II
Bird v Holbrook (1828)Spring gun set without warning injured trespasser.Disproportionate/private defence trap; excessive force not justified.Unit II
Limpus v London General Omnibus Co. (1862)Bus driver raced despite employer prohibition while driving bus.Employer may be liable for unauthorised mode of doing authorised work.Unit II
Joel v Morrison (1834)Servant substantially deviated from route for personal purpose.Frolic of own may take act outside course of employment.Unit II
Beard v London General Omnibus Co. (1900)Conductor drove bus without authority.Employer not liable where employee acts outside assigned function.Unit II
State of Rajasthan v Vidhyawati (1962)Government driver negligently caused fatal accident while driving government jeep.State liability for torts of employee in non-sovereign activity.Unit II
Honeywill & Stein Ltd v Larkin Bros (1934)Contractor’s dangerous work caused damage.Exception to general independent-contractor rule for inherently hazardous work.Unit II
Tubervell v Savage (1669)Words accompanying hand-on-sword gesture negated immediate threat.Words/context may negate assault.Unit IV
Stephens v Myers (1830)Defendant advanced threateningly but was stopped before contact.Assault: reasonable apprehension of imminent battery; no contact required.Unit IV
Cole v Turner (1704)Touching in anger.Direct hostile/unlawful touching may constitute battery.Unit IV
Bird v Jones (1845)Plaintiff blocked from proceeding in one direction but could go another way.False imprisonment requires total restraint within the relevant limits.Unit IV
Meering v Grahame-White Aviation (1919)Person confined by guards without knowing they were present.Awareness of confinement is not essential to false imprisonment.Unit IV
Bhim Singh v State of J&K (1985)Legislator unlawfully detained and prevented from attending assembly.Compensation for violation of personal liberty by State action.Unit IV
Sim v Stretch (1936)Defamatory meaning assessed objectively.Statement defamatory if it tends to lower plaintiff in estimation of right-thinking members of society.Unit IV
Hulton v Jones (1910)Fictional character shared name with real person.Intention to refer is not decisive if reasonable readers identify plaintiff.Unit IV
Theaker v Richardson (1962)Letter opened/read by third party.Publication to one third person is sufficient.Unit IV
Adam v Ward (1917)Official communication made in discharge of duty.Qualified privilege where duty/interest exists; malice may defeat it.Unit IV
Abrath v North Eastern Railway (1886)Malicious prosecution claim following criminal proceedings.Plaintiff must prove absence of reasonable and probable cause.Unit IV
Brown v Hawkes (1891)Prosecution supported by honest belief and reasonable grounds.Illustrates reasonable and probable cause.Unit IV
Rookes v Barnard (1964)Union officials threatened unlawful strike action causing employer to dismiss worker.Intimidation by unlawful threat causing loss.Unit IV
Quinn v Leathem (1901)Combination used coercive means to injure business.Conspiracy liability; predominant purpose and unlawful means distinctions matter.Unit IV
Entick v Carrington (1765)Officials entered and searched home under general warrant.Trespass to land; executive power must have legal authority.Unit IV
Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha (1995)Paid medical treatment considered under consumer law.Medical services for consideration fall within consumer protection; 2024 Supreme Court referral for larger Bench reconsideration should be noted.Unit V
Lucknow Development Authority v M.K. Gupta (1994)Consumer complaint against development authority for deficient housing services.Public authorities providing services may be liable under consumer law.Unit V
Spring Meadows Hospital v Harjol Ahluwalia (1998)Child suffered serious harm due to medical negligence.Medical negligence may constitute deficiency in service; consumer standing/compensation.Unit V
Sarla Verma v Delhi Transport Corporation (2009)Supreme Court standardised multiplier approach in motor accident death compensation.Structured multiplier method for just compensation.Unit V
T.O. Anthony v Karvarnan (2008)Supreme Court distinguished contributory and composite negligence.Contributory negligence reduces claimant’s recovery; composite negligence concerns multiple wrongdoers and innocent claimant.Unit II/III
Khenyei v New India Assurance Co. (2015)Claim arising from composite negligence of multiple vehicles.In composite negligence, claimant may recover full compensation from joint tortfeasors; inter se contribution is separate.Unit II/III
