Unit I — Voter denied vote but preferred candidate wins

IRAC: Issue: Is there actionable injury without financial loss? Rule: Injuria sine damno; legal-right violation is actionable. Application: Denial of qualified vote violates a legal right. Conclusion: Claim is actionable; Ashby v White.

Unit I — Competitor opens a cheaper school and causes loss of pupils

IRAC: Issue: Is economic loss alone actionable? Rule: Damnum sine injuria; lawful competition without infringement of right is not actionable. Application: No unlawful right was violated. Conclusion: ordinarily no tort; Gloucester Grammar School Case.

Unit II — Employee sees workplace risk but continues due to job pressure

IRAC: Issue: Does knowledge equal consent? Rule: Volenti requires free and voluntary acceptance, not mere knowledge. Application: Economic pressure may negate voluntariness. Conclusion: defence may fail; Smith v Baker.

Unit II — Servant makes prohibited manoeuvre while performing employer’s task

IRAC: Issue: Is employer vicariously liable? Rule: authorised task performed in unauthorised manner may remain within course of employment. Application: Assess purpose and connection. Conclusion: possible employer liability; Limpus v London General Omnibus.

Unit III — Barrel falls from warehouse onto pedestrian

IRAC: Issue: Can negligence be inferred? Rule: Res ipsa where event ordinarily implies negligence, control and no better explanation. Application: Warehouse barrel likely within defendant control. Conclusion: inference may arise; Byrne v Boadle.

Unit III — Factory stores hazardous gas; leak injures nearby residents

IRAC: Issue: Strict or absolute liability? Rule: Rylands has elements and exceptions; Indian absolute liability applies to hazardous enterprise without Rylands exceptions. Application: hazardous industrial activity and leak. Conclusion: apply M.C. Mehta principle.

Unit IV — Threatening gesture causes fear but no physical contact

IRAC: Issue: Assault or battery? Rule: Assault is reasonable apprehension of imminent battery; battery requires unlawful contact. Application: no contact, apparent immediate ability. Conclusion: assault may be established, not battery.

Unit IV — Person blocked from one path but can leave by another open route

IRAC: Issue: False imprisonment? Rule: total restraint required. Application: alternative route means not total confinement. Conclusion: no false imprisonment; Bird v Jones.

Unit V — Consumer pays ₹35 lakh for defective appliance; complaint filed after 18 months

IRAC: Issue: forum and limitation under CPA 2019? Rule: District Commission jurisdiction up to ₹50 lakh consideration paid; two-year limitation subject to condonation. Application: ₹35 lakh is within District Commission threshold; 18 months within limitation. Conclusion: District Commission, subject to other jurisdiction requirements.

Unit V — Road accident causes grievous hurt; claimant cannot prove driver negligence

IRAC: Issue: no-fault statutory route? Rule: Section 164 provides ₹2.5 lakh for grievous hurt without proof of wrongful act/neglect/default. Application: grievous hurt due to use of motor vehicle. Conclusion: Section 164 route may apply; check statutory definitions and claim procedure.
