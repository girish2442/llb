Applicable syllabus basis

The user’s admission batch is 2024–25. KSLU’s 2018–19 revised 3-year LL.B. syllabus lists Law of Torts in Semester I. The Board of Studies (UG) revision dated 24 March 2021 replaced Consumer Protection Act, 1986 with Consumer Protection Act, 2019 in Unit V from academic year 2021–22. Thus the 2024–25 batch must prepare CPA 2019, while Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 remains with later amendments.

The uploaded KSLU_LAW_OF_TORTS_SYLLABUS.docx and coverage tracker still reproduce CPA 1986. They are now flagged as legacy/outdated on Unit V. The unit headings I–IV remain aligned with the KSLU unit structure.

Unit V — critical legal correction

  • CPA 1986 replaced by CPA 2019 in KSLU Unit V revision effective 2021–22.
  • CPA 2019: District Commission up to ₹50 lakh; State above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore; National above ₹2 crore (value of consideration paid), under 2021 Jurisdiction Rules.
  • CPA 2019 Section 69: two-year complaint limitation, subject to condonation.
  • Motor Vehicles Act Section 164: ₹5 lakh for death and ₹2.5 lakh for grievous hurt without proving fault.
  • Hit-and-run scheme from 1 April 2022: ₹2 lakh for death; ₹50,000 for grievous hurt.
  • 2024 Supreme Court reference in Bar of Indian Lawyers matter puts a qualification on IMA v Shantha; it has not simply been overruled by that reference.

Case-law verification notes

  • Unit III source list “Winterbottom v Lord Derby (1867)” is not the recognised authority commonly intended; the classic case is Winterbottom v Wright (1842). Check before memorising.
  • Davies v Mann is a historical last-opportunity authority, not a complete statement of modern contributory negligence. Add Indian apportionment/composite-negligence cases.
  • Mayhem is a historical category; no case was invented where source notes explicitly lacked a leading authority.
  • Indian Medical Association v V.P. Shantha (1995) remains an important syllabus case but was referred to a larger Bench for reconsideration in 2024.
